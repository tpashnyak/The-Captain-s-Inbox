The Captain's Inbox

A four-round simulation about the professional courtesy nobody teaches: acknowledging the message. Students feel the cost of silence, write a 60-second reply, triage a ship's inbox, and set a team policy.

20–50 minutesPairs or small groupsNo login · nothing saved
Set sail

Five stops, one lesson: acknowledge the message.

Pick any single round for a 10-minute activity, or run all five in a 50-minute class. Project the page and click through, or send students the link to work in pairs.

Warm Up · 3 minutes

You email a company with a question about a $4,500 purchase. How long will you wait for any reply before you try a competitor? Have students hold up fingers: 1 = one hour, 2 = one day, 3 = two days, 4 = a week. Record the class median on the board and return to it in Round 4.

Teacher guide and CED connections

By the end of this lesson, you will learn:

  • How silence affects customer trust, sales, and daily operations
  • The three parts of a professional acknowledgment: confirm receipt, show courtesy, set a next step
  • How to prioritize an inbox and set expectations when you are unavailable
  • How a response-time standard works as a key performance indicator (KPI) that balances customers and employee well-being

Pacing

  • Quick run (20 min): Warm Up, Round 1 (one scenario as a class), Round 2
  • Full lesson (50 min): Warm Up 3 · Round 1 10 · Round 2 8 · Round 3 12 · Round 4 12 · Reflect 5
  • Prep: none. Every round has built-in feedback. Copy buttons let students paste work into your LMS.

Round 2 · The 60-Second Lifeboat

You can't answer everything yet. Acknowledge it anyway.

Pick a message, start the clock, and write a reply that confirms receipt, shows courtesy, and sets a realistic next step. The checklist updates as you type. No 12-page report required.
